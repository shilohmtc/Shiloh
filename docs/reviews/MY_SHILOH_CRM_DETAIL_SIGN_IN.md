# My Shiloh CRM-detail sign-in — review draft

Owner direction on 9 October 2026: ordinary sign-in may match first name, surname, DOB and phone against CRM without SMS. A saved passkey stays optional, with CRM fallback. This is a deliberate acceptance of biographical impersonation risk, not secure identity or phone-ownership verification. It does not implement an account lock or biometric app unlock.

## Draft behavior

Exact normalized South African phone, complete name and DOB must match one active record. New explicit name fields match individually. Legacy records retain their original single full name: compare the supplied first name plus surname against that full value, with NFC, case and whitespace normalization only. Do not guess a surname split, remove punctuation/accents, infer DOB/gender, or backfill existing records. Missing DOB, ambiguous records, archived-phone collisions and mismatches fail generically; retries never auto-register a missed sign-in.

Explicit registration requires separate first name and surname, DOB, gender and phone. Gender includes Prefer not to say and is never proof. Existing matching records are reused without updating any submitted values. Canonical phone collisions and exact normalized name/DOB collisions prevent new records. First/surname fields are stored only for explicitly supplied new names; a later authorized full-name edit clears those fields instead of guessing another split. Registration writes mobile_verified_at=NULL and self-reported provenance, preserving voucher/welcome-voucher ownership-verification checks. No other CRM record is silently merged or overwritten.

CRM sessions use auth_method=crm_details and assurance=biographical_match. New sessions have the agreed fixed 30-day deadline; cookies cannot outlive it. All previously stored deadlines remain unchanged. Activity, polling and CSRF rotation do not refresh expiry or recent authentication. Strong SMS/passkey/recovery issuance stays separate. Credential enrollment/removal, recovery creation and other-session revocation require a recognized strong method within ten minutes, plus a stored-session revocation/expiry/assurance recheck. Profile identity edits require fresh strong proof and existing verified-mobile/revision/transaction controls. Ordinary logout works for CRM sessions. Staff permissions, WebAuthn verification, installation gate, migration 188 and SMS budgets remain intact.

## Abuse controls and remaining limits

A shared transactional limiter counts malformed and successful attempts too, across replicas/restarts. Identity: 6/15 minutes and 40/day; device: 20/15 minutes; network: 100/15 minutes (IPv6 /64); clinic: 1000/15 minutes and 5000/day. Identity/device attempts have bounded exponential backoff after the third attempt. Windows never roll forward on activity. These are new CRM controls, not changes to SMS budgets. Limits and rate-key rotation need review before activation. All limiter keys are HMACs; failed audits contain no submitted names, DOB, phone, network address, candidate IDs or match reason. Database diagnostics are reduced to a schema/database category. Public failures give no field hints, candidates or existence status, with a common minimum response time.

Residual risk: anyone who knows the required CRM details can obtain that client's ordinary private session, including whatever existing booking/consultation/voucher/reward authorities permit. Optional passkeys do not stop CRM fallback. Rate limiting cannot turn biographical data into identity proof. Immediate successful registration also signals that a supplied phone could be registered; generic errors cannot fully remove this availability signal. Mistyping both phone and name may evade exact duplicate detection; fuzzy automatic merging is deliberately excluded. These limitations require independent release review; this draft must not claim enumeration prevention is absolute.
